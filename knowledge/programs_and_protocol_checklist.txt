TITLE: VISIT PROGRAMS AND PROTOCOL CHECKLISTS

SECTION 1. PURPOSE

A visit program is the structured schedule of an official visit.
It defines:
- time
- place
- participants
- sequence
- protocol transitions
- ceremonial logic

A protocol checklist is the operational control tool that ensures every required element is prepared.

Both are essential.

SECTION 2. VISIT PROGRAM PRINCIPLES

A strong visit program should be:
- realistic
- clear
- timed properly
- not overloaded
- protocol-aware
- logistically workable
- easy to brief

Programs should include enough detail for execution, but not so much clutter that they become unreadable.

SECTION 3. COMMON VISIT ELEMENTS

Official visits may include:
- arrival
- greeting and reception
- transfer
- photo moment
- bilateral meeting
- expanded delegation meeting
- signing ceremony
- official lunch or dinner
- tour
- press statement
- farewell
- departure

SECTION 4. BASIC PROGRAM STRUCTURE

A clean program usually follows:

TIME
EVENT
LOCATION
PARTICIPANTS
NOTES

Example:
10:00 Arrival of Delegation
10:15 Greeting at Main Entrance
10:20 Photo Opportunity
10:30 Bilateral Meeting
11:15 Expanded Meeting
12:15 Gift Exchange
12:30 Official Lunch
14:00 Departure

SECTION 5. TRANSITION LOGIC

Transitions between segments are critical.

The protocol officer must ensure:
- room is ready before arrival
- greeting line is in position
- transport is ready before exit
- media is in the correct place before principals appear
- next room is prepared before the current segment ends

SECTION 6. ONE-DAY OFFICIAL VISIT

A one-day visit should prioritize:
- principal meeting
- core political or institutional purpose
- controlled ceremonial elements
- enough time for movement and interpretation
- reasonable end time

Do not overload a one-day visit with too many symbolic events.

SECTION 7. AIRPORT OR ARRIVAL PROTOCOL

If arrival is formal, checklist should include:
- exact arrival time
- flight details if relevant
- who meets the delegation
- vehicle sequence
- flag use
- escort
- luggage and VIP route
- media presence if any

SECTION 8. BUILDING ARRIVAL

At building arrival, checklist should include:
- entrance route
- receiving line
- principal waiting position
- photo point
- delegation holding position
- security clearance
- weather contingency if outdoors

SECTION 9. MEETING CHECKLIST

For the meeting room:
- seating chart
- nameplates
- flags
- water and glasses
- briefing folders
- microphones
- interpreters
- pens and notepads
- room temperature and lighting
- press access instructions

SECTION 10. OFFICIAL LUNCH OR DINNER CHECKLIST

For meals:
- seating plan
- menu
- dietary restrictions
- host and guest place cards
- speech or toast sequence
- interpreters if needed
- gift placement if linked to meal
- timing for transition from meeting to dining room

SECTION 11. PRESS CHECKLIST

Press coordination should confirm:
- whether press is present
- where they stand
- whether handshake photo is planned
- whether there is a statement
- whether only opening minutes are covered
- who escorts media in and out

SECTION 12. GIFT EXCHANGE CHECKLIST

If gifts are exchanged:
- confirm gift exists physically
- confirm who carries it
- confirm timing
- confirm order of exchange
- confirm photo moment if needed

SECTION 13. DEPARTURE CHECKLIST

Before departure:
- vehicles positioned
- principals guided correctly
- final handshake sequence clear
- no staff confusion in corridor or entrance
- luggage and escort managed where applicable

SECTION 14. CHECKLIST STRUCTURE

A practical checklist should separate categories:

A. PRINCIPALS
B. DELEGATION
C. ROOM
D. FLAGS AND NAMEPLATES
E. INTERPRETATION
F. PRESS
G. GIFTS
H. TRANSPORT
I. CATERING
J. DEPARTURE

SECTION 15. COMMON PROGRAM ERRORS

Typical errors:
- unrealistic timing
- too many segments
- no transition buffer
- no interpreter timing allowance
- media conflict with movement
- room not ready
- lunch too early or too late
- travel time underestimated
- unclear farewell logic

SECTION 16. PROTOCOLMIND OUTPUT STANDARD

When asked to create visit programs or checklists, ProtocolMind should:
- use realistic timing
- organize the sequence clearly
- separate operational and ceremonial elements
- include protocol notes where useful
- avoid vague filler
- produce text that can be used operationally
